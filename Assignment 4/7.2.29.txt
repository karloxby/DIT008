7.2.29.		Define H: ℝ × ℝ → ℝ × ℝ as follows:
		H(x, y) = (x + 1, 2 - y) for every (x, y) ∈ ℝ × ℝ.

		a.	Is H injective? Prove or give a counterexample.

		b.	Is H surjective? Prove or give a counterexample.


a.	Is H injective? Prove or give a counterexample.

	Proof:

	Suppose H(x, y) = (x + 1, 2 - y).

	We must show whether H is injective or not.

	Definition of injective:

		Outputs cannot be shared

	Let (x₁, y₁), (x₂, y₂) ∈ ℝ × ℝ

	Suppose H(x₁, y₁) = H(x₂, y₂), then x₁ + 1 = x₂ + 1
	and 2 - y₁ = 2 - y₂.

		x₁ + 1 = x₂ + 1
		(x₁ + 1) - 1 = (x₂ + 1) - 1
		x₁ = x₂

		2 - y₁ = 2 - y₂
		(2 - y₁) - 2 = (2 - y₂) - 2
		-y₁ = -y₂
		y₁ = y₂
	
	Hence, x₁ = x₂ and y₁ = y₂. Therefore, (x₁, y₁) = (x₂, y₂),
	which is what was to be shown.

								□

Solution:	H is injective.


b.	Is H surjective? Prove or give a counterexample.

	Proof:

	Suppose H(x, y) = (x + 1, 2 - y).

	We must show whether H is surjective or not.

	Definition of surjective:

		Outputs cannot go unused
	
	Let (m, n) ∈ ℝ × ℝ

	Suppose H(x, y) = (m, n), then (x + 1, 2 - y) = (m, n).

	Hence, m = x + 1, and n = 2 - y

		x = m - 1
		y = 2 - n

	Since H(x, y) = (x + 1, 2 - y), then:
	
		H(m - 1, 2 - n) = ((m - 1) + 1, 2 - (2 - n)) = (m, n)

	Therefore, H(x, y) = (m, n), which is what was to be
	shown.

								□

Solution:	H is surjective.
