Let P(X) and Q(x) be predicates and suppose D is the
domain of x. In 55-58, for the statement forms in each
pair, determine whether (a) they have the same truth
value for every choice of P(x), Q(x) and D, or (b) there is
a choice of P(x), Q(x), and D for which they have opposite
truth values.


57.	(A)x in D, (P(x) v Q(x)), and ((A)x in D, P(x)) v ((A)x in D, Q(x))

	(A)x in D, (P(x) v Q(x))

	In English:

	"For all x in D, P(x) or Q(x)."

	((A)x in D, P(x)) v ((A)x in D, Q(x))

	In English:

	"For all x in D, P(x), or For all x in D, Q(x)."

	Let's revisit the conditions for (a) and (b):
	
		(a)	Both statement forms share the same truth
			value for every choice of P(x), Q(x) and D

		(b)	There is a choice of P(x), Q(x) and D, for
			which they have the opposite truth values.

	Let D = {0, 1}, and 
	let P(x) : x = 0, and
	let Q(x) : x = 1.
	
	In the first statement form, we are looking
	for where P(x) or Q(x) is true for all x.

	(A)x in D, (P(x) v Q(x))

	
	x	P(x)	Q(x)	P(x) v Q(x)
	----------------------------------
	0	T	F	T
	1	F	T	T


	Whereas, in the second statement form, we are
	looking for where P(x) is true for all x or where
	Q(x) is true for all x.

	((A)x in D, P(x)) v ((A)x in D, Q(x))


	x	P(x)	Q(x)
	-------------------
	0	T	F
	1	F	T


	The truth table for the first statement form tells
	us that the condition can be true for P(x) or Q(x).

	(A)x in D, (P(x) v Q(x)) = True

	However, the truth table for the second statement
	form tells us that the condition is neither true
	for P(x) or Q(x).

	((A)x in D, P(x)) v ((A)x in D, Q(x)) = False

	In which case, upon comparison, we can conclude that
	the two statement forms can have the opposite truth 
	values.

	Therefore, option (b) is the correct answer
	for the given statement forms.


Solution:	(b) 	There is a choice of P(x), Q(x) and D
			for which they have opposite truth values.
