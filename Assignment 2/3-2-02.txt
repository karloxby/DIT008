2.	Which of the following is a negation for "All dogs
	are loyal"? More than one answer may be correct.

	a.	All dogs are disloyal.
	b.	No dogs are loyal.
	c.	Some dogs are disloyal.
	d.	Some dogs are loyal.
	e.	There is a disloyal animal that is not a dog.

	
	let d = Is a dog, and
	let l = Is loyal

	Let's look at the statement:

	"All dogs are loyal."

	Since the statement clearly claims that all dogs are
	loyal, we can consider the statement to be universal,
	and the negation of a universal statement is an
	existential statement, which can be written as:

	"There is at least one dog that is not loyal."

	Now, if we look at the options a through e again:

	a.	All dogs are disloyal.
	b.	No dogs are loyal.
	c.	Some dogs are disloyal.
	d.	Some dogs are loyal.
	e.	There is a disloyal animal that is not a dog.

	We can check which option fits the negation best.

	b.	Some dogs are disloyal.
	c.	Some dogs are loyal.

	Both of these statements imply that there are both
	loyal and disloyal dogs, which corresponds with our
	negation of the given statement.


Solution:	b and c are negations of the given statement.
