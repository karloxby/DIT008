In 35-39 find the mistakes in the "proofs" that the sum
of any two rational numbers is a rational number.


36.	"Proof: Let rational numbers r = 1/4 and s = 1/2 be
	given. Then r + s = 1/4 + 1/2 = 3/4, which is a rational
	number. This is what was to be shown."


	Let's first write our own proof:

	Proof:
		Assume r is 1/4, and s = 1/2, and that r and s are
		rational numbers.

		We must prove that r + s is a rational numbers.

		First, we know that according to the defintion of
		a rational number, a rational number has to be
		the quotient of two integers.

			1, 2 and 4 are all integers.
	
		Therefore, both r and s are rational numbers.

		Let's look at the theorem of the sum of two
		rational numbers next:

		Let a, b, c, d, p, q be integers, and b ≠ 0
		and d ≠ 0.
			
			r + s =	(a/b) + (c/d)
		  	    =	(ad + bc) / (bd)

		p = ad + bc and q = bd, then p and q
		are integers, because products and sums of
		integers are also integers. Also q ≠ 0 by the
		zero product property.

		Thus, r + s = p / q, where p and q are integers
		and q ≠ 0.

		Therefore, r + is is rational by definition of
		a rational number, as was to be shown.
							(Proof)
	
	Now that we have our own proof, we can compare to the
	given proof. The mistake of the proof is that it's
	not a proof whether they are rational, but more so
	a pure calculation.


Solution:	The mistake is that it is not a proof. It is
		a calculation. It only proves for one specific
		pair, but the given statement was for any sum
		of rational numbers.
