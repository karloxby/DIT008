36.	The difference of the squares of any two
	consecutive integers is odd


	Let's first assess what we're dealing with here
	before we make any assumptions.

	We are looking for any two consecutive integers. 
	They are consecutive, because they follow each 
	other on the number axis, e.g. 1 and 2.

	Then we want to square these numbers, e.g. 1**2
	and 2**2.

	And in order for the statement to be true, the
	difference of the two squares should be odd,
	i.e. 1**2 - 2**2 is odd.

	I think we can prove that this is true.

	Proof:

		Suppose that we have any two consecutive
		integers, and their respective squares.

		We have to prove that the difference of
		the squares is odd.

		The definition of odd according to Epp is:

			"n is odd <=> n = 2k + 1 for some
			integer k"

		And the definition of even according to Epp
		is:

			"n is even <=> n = 2k for some
			integer k"

			Let a in Z
			b = a + 1

			b**2 = (a + 1)**2 = a**2 + 2a + 1

			a**2 - b**2 = a**2 - (a**2 + 2a + 1) =
			= a**2 - a**2 - 2a - 1 = -2a - 1

			-2a - 1 = 2(-a - 1) + 1

		Since 2(-a - 1) + 1 fulfills the definition
		of an odd number, where -a - 1 is an integer,
		we have proven that the statement is true.

		Therefore, the difference of the squares
		of any two consecutive integers is odd.


Solution:	The statement is true.
