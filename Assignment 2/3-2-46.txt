Use the facts that the negation of a (A) statement is
a (E) statement and that the negation of an if-then
statement is an and statement to rewrite each of the
statements 45-48 without using the word necessary or
sufficient.


46.	Having a large income is not a necessary condi-
	tion for a person to be happy.

	Let's look at the definition of a necessary condition
	according to Epp:

	"(A)x, r(x) is a necessary condition for s(x)" means
	"(A)x, if ~r(x) then ~s(x)" or, equivalently,
	"(A)x, if s(x) then r(x)"

	Let's assign predicates from the initial statement:
		
		Let x be a person, and 
		let r(x) = x has a large income, and 
		let s(x) = x is happy
	
	Epp gives us (A)x, if s(x) then r(x), which in English
	means:

		"For all x, if x is happy then x has a large income"

		which can be written as

		(A)x, if s(x) then r(x)

	But the wording of the initial statement states that r(x) is
	NOT a necessary condition for s(x).

	So what we're essentially trying to say, is that there is
	at least one person that is happy, but does not have a 
	large income.

	This is an existential statement. And by definition, if we negate 
	a universal statement, we get an existential statement.

	Therefore, we negate the statement as such:

		(1)	~((A)x, if s(x) then r(x))

		(2)	~((A)x) = (E)x s.t.

		(3)	~(if s(x) then r(x)) = s(x) and ~r(x)

			(E)x s.t. s(x) ^ ~r(x)
			
			In English:

			"There is at least one person, such that
			the person is happy, and does not have
			a large income."
	
	This statement now fulfills the condition of the assignment,
	and we can therefore conclude that we are done.


Solution:	(E)x s.t. s(x) ^ ~r(x)
