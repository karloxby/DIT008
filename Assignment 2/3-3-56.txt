Let P(X) and Q(x) be predicates and suppose D is the
domain of x. In 55-58, for the statement forms in each
pair, determine whether (a) they have the same truth
value for every choice of P(x), Q(x) and D, or (b) there is
a choice of P(x), Q(x), and D for which they have opposite
truth values.


56.	(E)x in D s.t. P(x) ^ Q(x) and ((E)x in D s.t. P(x)) ^ ((E)x in D s.t. Q(x))

	(E)x in D s.t. P(x) ^ Q(x)

	In English:

	"There is at least one x in D, such that
	P(x) and Q(x)."

	((E)x in D s.t. P(x)) ^ ((E)x in D s.t. Q(x))

	In English:

	"There is at least one x in D, such that P(x)
	and there is at least one x in D, such that Q(x)."

	Let's revisit the conditions for (a) and (b):
	
		(a)	Both statement forms share the same truth
			value for every choice of P(x), Q(x) and D

		(b)	There is a choice of P(x), Q(x) and D, for
			which they have the opposite truth values.

	Let D = {0, 1}, and 
	let P(x) : x = 0, and
	let Q(x) : x = 1.

	In the first statement form, we are looking
	for a row where both P(x) and Q(x) are true.

	(E)x in D s.t. P(x) ^ Q(x)


	x	P(x)	Q(x)	P(x) ^ Q(x)
	----------------------------------
	0	T	F	F
	1	F	T	F	  
	

	(E)x in D s.t. P(x) ^ Q(x) is false, since
	there exists no value of x where both P(x)
	and Q(x) is true.

	(E)x in D s.t. P(x) ^ Q(x) = False

	However, in the second statement form, we are
	looking for a true value of P(x) and a true
	value of Q(x) in any row.

	((E)x in D s.t. P(x)) ^ ((E)x in D s.t. Q(x))


	x	P(x)	Q(x)	
	-------------------
	0	T	F
	1	F	T

	
	((E)x in D s.t. P(x)) ^ ((E)x in D s.t. Q(x))
	is true, since there exists a value of x where
	P(x) can be true and Q(x) can be true.

	((E)x in D s.t. P(x)) ^ ((E)x in D s.t. Q(x)) = True

	In which case, upon comparison, we can conclude that
	the two statement forms can have the opposite truth 
	values.

	Therefore, option (b) is the correct answer
	for the given statement forms.


Solution:	(b) 	There is a choice of P(x), Q(x) and D
			for which they have opposite truth values.
