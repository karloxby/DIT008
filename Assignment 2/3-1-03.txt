3.	Let R(m, n) be the predicate "If m is a factor n**2
	then m is a factor of n," with domain for both m
	and n being Z the set of integers.

	a.	Explain why R(m, n) is false if m = 25 and
		n = 10.
		
	
	b.	Give values different from those in part (a) for
		which R(m, n) is false.

	c.	Explain why R(m, n) is true if m = 5 and
		n = 10.
	
	d.	Give values different from those in part (a) for
		which R(m, n) is true.
	

	Let R(m, n) in Z, and
	R(m, n) be m | n**2, then m | n.

a.	Explain why R(m, n) is false if m = 25 and
	n = 10.


	m = 25
	n = 10

	n**2 = 10**2 = 100

	25 | 100 -> 25 | 10

	100 / 25 = 4 in Z
	10 / 25 = 2 / 5 = 0.4 not in Z


Solution:	For m = 25 and n = 10, R(m, n) is false
		because (10**2) / 25 = 4 in Z, whereas
		10 / 25 = 0.4 not in Z.
	

b.	Give values different from those in part (a) for
	which R(m, n) is false.


	m = 25
	n = 5

	(5**2) / 25 = 25 / 25 = 1 in Z
	5 / 25 = 1 / 5 = 0.2 not in Z


Solution:	For m = 25 and n = 5, R(m, n) is false
		because (5**2) / 25 = 1 in Z, whereas
		5 / 25 = 0.2 not in Z.

	
c.	Explain why R(m, n) is true if m = 5 and
	n = 10.


	m = 5
	n = 10

	(10**2) / 5 = 100 / 5 = 20 in Z
	10 / 5 = 2 in Z


Solution:	For m = 5 and n = 10, R(m, n) is true
		because (10**2) / 5 = 20 in Z, and
		10 / 5 = 2 in Z.


d.	Give values different from those in part (a) for
	which R(m, n) is true.


	m = 3
	n = 9

	(9**2) / 3 = 81 / 3 = 27 in Z
	9 / 3 = 3 in Z


Solution:	For m = 3 and n = 9, R(m, n) is true
		because (9**2) / 3 = 27 in Z, and
		9 / 3 = 3 in Z.
