		a.	Use the quotient-remainder theorem with
			divisor equal to 3 to prove that the product
			of any two consecutive integers has the form
			3k or 3k + 2 for some integer k.

		Suppose k is some integer.

		We must show using the quotient-remainder theorem
		with divisor equal to 3, that any two consecutive
		integers has the form 3k or 3k + 2 for k.
		
		The quotient-remainder theorem:

		"∀n ∈ ℤ, and ∀d ∈ ℤ⁺, ∃q, r ∈ ℤ st n = dq + r
		and 0 ≤ r < d"

		In English:

		"For all integers n, and for all positive integers
		d, there are some integers q and r, such that
		n is dq + r, and r is greater than or equal to 0,
		and less than d."
